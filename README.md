# Between the Lines

An interactive miniature Barcelona metro interchange for estimating transfer time. The default Verdaguer scene starts with a 200 m walk, 30 m escalator, 12 m of stairs, and 35 m moving walkway. A narrow Barcelona street sits above two orthogonal L4/L5 platforms: the lower train passes underneath the upper line, and a separate passage connects them. The camera starts with the whole cutaway in view: drag to turn it and scroll to zoom.

## Run

Requires Node.js 20.19+ or 22.12+ (Node 24 is supported).

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev
```

Open the local address printed by Vite. Run `npm run build` to check the production bundle and `npm test` for the calculation and layout tests.

## How it works

Name the station and choose the two metro lines in the dock below the scene. The station name appears on the entrance; interior line signs attach to the track and passage walls. The line colors and train markings update immediately. The coloured line menus show every available line and support arrow keys, Home/End, Enter, and Escape. The dock has separate Calculator, Presets, and Validate while there tabs. The Presets library lists station examples alphabetically, with their line colours, distances, and estimates; route templates have their own subsection. Loading Verdaguer, Passeig de Gràcia, Diagonal, or Catalunya updates the name, line pair, lengths, and calculation, then returns to the calculator. These are clearly labelled illustrative lengths, not surveyed station data. Editing a loaded example clears its selected highlight. All four inputs are lengths in metres; blank fields count as zero. The estimate adds the four segment times using 1.3 m/s for walking, 0.5 m/s for standing on an escalator, 0.65 m/s on stairs, and 1.8 m/s for walking on a moving walkway (walking plus belt speed).

Tunnel length, escalator run and rise, and stair run and rise grow with their inputs. The passage has a generous minimum span and a pronounced response to walking distance; its curve still approaches a finite limit so extreme distances keep a usable miniature. The exposed upper railway extends with the passage to continue across the lower line, ending in a short hollow tunnel sleeve, and the streetscape fills the longer block with buildings. The fixed entrance lands on the upper platform, with fare gates across the walking direction at the foot of its stairs. Floor heights, landings, walking paths and train clearance envelopes share one layout model. Moving walkways appear only for positive inputs. Commuters use ramps onto the belt, accelerate while on it, and use a separate lane for the return journey. Calculating shows the estimate; the normal world keeps only its walking commuters. Trains enter and leave through hollow track tunnels; their clipping boundaries sit inside the tunnel sleeves. The upper train travels along the complete exposed track before disappearing inside its terminal sleeve, even at maximum passage length. The layout is a schematic interpretation rather than a measured station plan.

Open **Validate while there** and press **Start validation** when you begin walking between platforms. A larger cream marker with a red outline follows the route for the full calculated duration. Walking, escalator, stair, and moving-walkway sections each receive their calculated share of that time; the decorative commuters keep their own animation pace. The countdown and marker use elapsed wall time, so switching menu tabs or returning from a background browser tab does not restart the run. The marker stops at the destination without looping, with a checkered finish and a brief confetti celebration. Stop the run to clear it; editing a distance, station name, or line resets it. Zero-length routes cannot be started. This is a visual estimate to compare with your walk, not location tracking. Reduced-motion mode keeps the functional timer and marker but removes decorative pulsing and confetti.

Size changes ease over 800 milliseconds using bounded geometry controls. The platforms, passage, stairs, escalators, walkway, streetscape, and walking paths share the same intermediate layout, preserving their connections as the station grows. Camera fitting eases too and gives control back immediately when you drag or zoom. Reduced-motion mode applies size changes immediately.

Turn the model all the way around to discover the creators’ terrace, centred on the solid rear wall without covering the lower railway opening. The airport-photo composition has one friend reclining on a compact stone chair with a lower base and its backrest towards the wall, with both feet fully supported, and three friends standing behind. All four look upwards. Their clothes distinguish them: a navy zip hoodie with light stubble, a closed jacket with grey backpack straps, brown corduroy with a scarf, and a closed denim jacket with separate tan shoulder straps looping back to a backpack. A framed sunset uses a vertex-colour sky, a solid sun, and sculpted clouds. The larger Vueling aircraft flies opposite the friends’ gaze, with yellow engines and winglets, a dotted tail, and a grey-and-yellow wordmark. It rises above the frame and peeks over the Barcelona street on the initial view. `CreatorsNook.jsx`, `CreatorsPlane.jsx`, and `SunsetBackdrop.jsx` build the scene from real geometry; `creatorsLayout.js` keeps its positioning and camera bounds together. Scroll over a detail to zoom towards it; the camera supports up to four times its initial zoom and can turn down to a near-horizontal view.

The scene is built from React Three Fiber meshes in `src/world/`. `StationWorld.jsx` assembles the cutaway and fits the orbit camera to its geometry, `stationLayout.js` defines the dimensions and clearance envelopes, `Surface.jsx` builds the Barcelona streetscape and entrance, and `Architecture.jsx` supplies the structures. `Train.jsx` and `trainSchedule.js` model the train cycle; `journeyPaths.js` and `PeopleFlow.jsx` define and animate the commuter routes and calculation bubble. `lineCatalog.js` defines the line palette. `validationPath.js` assigns real transfer times to the route, `ValidationRoute.jsx` draws the marker and finish effects, and `useStationLayout.js` animates coherent size changes. `src/components/CalculatorPanel.jsx` is the tabbed HTML calculator dock; `LineSelect.jsx` provides its keyboard-accessible, portal-based line menus so they remain visible above the scrolling mobile dock. The tests check clearances, landings and walking routes across 256 input combinations, bounded growth, train/door timing, wall-clock validation timing, zero-duration sections, and clearances during size transitions. The renderer caps pixel density, uses ambient occlusion and contact shadows, and respects reduced-motion preferences. No external assets, accounts, or services are needed at runtime.
