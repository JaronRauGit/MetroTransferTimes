# Between the Lines

An interactive miniature Barcelona metro interchange for estimating transfer time. The calculator opens with a Diagonal example (L3 towards Trinitat Nova → L5 towards Cornellà Centre): the original 190.0563 m walking distance, 58 escalator steps, zero additional stair steps, and 71.28 m moving walkway. It starts without revealing a result. A narrow Barcelona street sits above two orthogonal L3/L5 platforms: the lower train passes underneath the upper line, and a separate passage connects them. The camera starts with the whole cutaway in view: drag to turn it and scroll to zoom.

## Run

Requires Node.js 20.19+ or 22.12+ (Node 24 is supported).

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev
```

Open the local address printed by Vite. Run `npm run build` to check the production bundle and `npm test` for the calculation and layout tests.

## How it works

The dock opens directly on **Calculate transfer time**, with the original four unit-labelled measurement fields. Measurements determine the estimate; station names and line choices label the miniature only. Edit those labels directly in the top-right corner, where the arriving and departing lines have coloured badges. The header remains available on mobile. Line menus support arrow keys, Home/End, Enter, and Escape. Method and real-time validation live in a separate Further information section.

The **Already mapped** shortcut opens a station chooser with **Sants Estació** and **Diagonal**, using measurements restored from [the original Second branch at commit 1a75db5](https://github.com/JaronRauGit/MetroTransferTimes/blob/1a75db52d1b2f31559a02e5b83fea19b97acd9e3/index.html#L148). There are no tabs or station dropdowns. Selecting a station opens a four-platform diagram: the left column selects the arriving line’s direction, and the right selects the departing line’s direction. Click either card within a column to change that endpoint. Only transfers between L3 and L5 are selectable. Swap reverses the transfer, exchanges the line columns, and loads the original reverse-direction measurements. A compact read-only measurement row shows the calculator inputs; the route is not repeated below the diagram. The calculate button has the same label, styling and position beneath the main tool in both views. The sidebar provides understated links to the other mapped station and back to the custom calculator, whose draft is preserved.

All 32 historical station/platform combinations remain unchanged in the source data and are checked against the original calculator; the interface exposes the 16 transfers between different lines. Direction-specific differences are preserved. “Towards” means the destination displayed on a train using that platform. Loading a mapped route updates the station, lines, measurements, and world together, and clears any previous estimate or preview. Selecting a route never calculates automatically. Within each mapped station, the shortest and longest measured passages span a wider visual range so platform changes produce a clear, smoothly animated difference. Custom measurements retain their existing bounded size curve; the extra mapped exaggeration does not alter measurements or transfer times. No illustrative presets or generic route templates are included.

Inputs and speeds match the original calculator: **walking distance in metres at 1.3 m/s; escalators in steps at 1.5 steps/s (count the stairs beside the escalator); stairs without an escalator in steps at 1.9 steps/s; moving-walkway length in metres at 0.5 m/s.** Exclude moving-walkway length from walking distance and avoid counting an escalator again as stairs. Presets load additional stair steps as zero, exactly as the original did. Blank fields count as zero. The total is the sum of each input divided by its speed. The scene compresses metres and step counts into bounded visual dimensions; it does not change the calculator’s units or results.

Tunnel length, escalator run and rise, and stair run and rise grow with their inputs. The passage has a generous minimum span and a pronounced response to walking distance; its curve still approaches a finite limit so extreme distances keep a usable miniature. The exposed upper railway extends with the passage to continue across the lower line, ending in a short hollow tunnel sleeve, and the streetscape fills the longer block with buildings. The fixed entrance lands on the upper platform, with fare gates across the walking direction at the foot of its stairs. Floor heights, landings, walking paths and train clearance envelopes share one layout model. Moving walkways appear only for positive inputs. Commuters use ramps onto the belt, accelerate while on it, and use a separate lane for the return journey. Calculating starts a short animated journey before revealing the estimate; the normal world keeps its walking commuters. Trains enter and leave through hollow track tunnels; their clipping boundaries sit inside the tunnel sleeves. The upper train travels along the complete exposed track before disappearing inside its terminal sleeve, even at maximum passage length. The layout is a schematic interpretation rather than a measured station plan.

Press **Calculate transfer time** in either view to play a 9.7-second preview. The dock contracts into a loading screen showing only “Simulating your connection”, playful status messages, and a progress bar. The environment smoothly turns monochrome, existing trains fade, and ambient activity accelerates up to 20×. The camera eases towards the interchange at a slightly shallower angle. A coloured source train stops and opens its doors. Each preview randomly chooses a man or woman, retaining that character throughout the run. Both wear the same orange jacket and backpack, with a tied-back hairstyle and a slightly different jacket silhouette distinguishing the woman. The featured passenger appears with the stopwatch at zero for half a second before walking and counting begin together. The connected platform-to-platform journey takes five seconds, with a fading footprint trail. The passenger raises both arms while riding a moving walkway.

The coloured destination train collects the passenger and leaves before colour returns. The final estimate appears only in the dock, with a brief entrance and emphasis animation and a replay action. The station and route are not repeated beside the answer, and there is no destination result bubble. Background trains yield their track while each featured train occupies it. The camera returns to the previous view afterward. Changing measurements, labels or mapped routes clears stale results. Zero-time custom calculations and reduced-motion mode reveal the result immediately; reduced-motion also removes the result animation. Playful loading messages do not add delays to the estimate. The preview is separate from real-time validation and does not change any measurement or calculated duration.

Commuters share articulated person components with varied heights, skin tones, clothing, hair, hats, bags and phone poses. Their arms and legs swing, bodies sway and heads glance while waiting; small lane variations break up straight procession lines. Above ground, pedestrians walk both directions along continuous paths around the entrance, including a phone-checking pause. Two helmeted cyclists pedal along a marked cycle lane, with an occasional small car on a separate lane. Walking and cycling dominate the street activity. Street actors fade at the block edges between trips. Trains, doors, escalators, walkways and commuters use a shared continuous world clock so fast-forwarding the surroundings does not affect the highlighted passenger or real-time validator.

Open **Validate at the station** in Further information and press **Start validation** when you begin walking between platforms. A larger cream marker with a red outline follows the route for the full calculated duration. Walking, escalator, stair, and moving-walkway sections each receive their calculated share of that time; the decorative commuters keep their own animation pace. The countdown and marker use elapsed wall time, so returning from a background browser tab does not restart the run. The marker stops at the destination without looping, with a checkered finish and a brief confetti celebration. Stop the run to clear it; editing a distance, station name, or line resets it. Zero-length routes cannot be started. This is a visual estimate to compare with your walk, not location tracking. Reduced-motion mode keeps the functional timer and marker but removes decorative pulsing and confetti.

Size changes ease over 800 milliseconds using bounded geometry controls. The platforms, passage, stairs, escalators, walkway, streetscape, and walking paths share the same intermediate layout, preserving their connections as the station grows. Camera fitting eases too and gives control back immediately when you drag or zoom. Reduced-motion mode applies size changes immediately.

Turn the model all the way around to discover the creators’ terrace, centred on the solid rear wall without covering the lower railway opening. The airport-photo composition has one friend reclining on a compact stone chair with a lower base and its backrest towards the wall, with both feet fully supported, and three friends standing behind. All four look upwards. Their clothes distinguish them: a navy zip hoodie with light stubble, a closed jacket with grey backpack straps, brown corduroy with a scarf, and a closed denim jacket with separate tan shoulder straps looping back to a backpack. A framed sunset uses a vertex-colour sky, a solid sun, and sculpted clouds. The larger Vueling aircraft flies opposite the friends’ gaze, with yellow engines and winglets, a dotted tail, and a grey-and-yellow wordmark. It rises above the frame and peeks over the Barcelona street on the initial view. `CreatorsNook.jsx`, `CreatorsPlane.jsx`, and `SunsetBackdrop.jsx` build the scene from real geometry; `creatorsLayout.js` keeps its positioning and camera bounds together. Scroll over a detail to zoom towards it; the camera supports up to four times its initial zoom and can turn down to a near-horizontal view.

The scene is built from React Three Fiber meshes in `src/world/`. `StationWorld.jsx` assembles the cutaway and fits the orbit camera to its geometry, `stationLayout.js` defines the dimensions and clearance envelopes, `Surface.jsx` builds the Barcelona streetscape and entrance, and `Architecture.jsx` supplies the structures. `Train.jsx` and `trainSchedule.js` model the train cycle; `journeyPaths.js` and `PeopleFlow.jsx` define and animate the commuter routes. `lineCatalog.js` defines the line palette. `validationPath.js` assigns real transfer times to the route, `ValidationRoute.jsx` draws the marker and finish effects, and `useStationLayout.js` animates coherent size changes. `src/components/CalculatorPanel.jsx` provides the calculator and station chooser, `MappedPlanner.jsx` handles the graphical platform picker, and `SimulationLoading.jsx` masks controls during previews; `SceneLabels.jsx` provides the editable scene labels, and `LineSelect.jsx` provides keyboard-accessible, portal-based line menus so they remain visible above the scrolling mobile dock. `simulation.js` defines the preview choreography; `useCalculation.js` controls delayed results; `TransferSimulation.jsx` and `previewPath.js` stage the featured journey, and `JourneyLabels.jsx` projects the passenger stopwatch into the scene. `StreetMobility.jsx` and `streetPaths.js` provide the walking, cycling, and occasional car trips. `WorldMotion.jsx` provides the accelerated decorative clock and `MonochromeWorld.jsx` desaturates lit materials and textures while leaving featured objects in colour. A preserved original HTML snapshot in `test/fixtures/original-calculator.html` runs the original selection and calculation functions to verify every restored route and its validation duration. The tests also check preview/door timing, focus/background separation, preview walking clearances, landings and walking routes across 256 input combinations, bounded growth, train/door timing, wall-clock validation timing, zero-duration sections, clearances during size transitions, and street-path separation from the entrance and vehicle lanes. The renderer caps pixel density, uses ambient occlusion and contact shadows, and respects reduced-motion preferences. No external assets, accounts, or services are needed at runtime.
